## 1. What was built

The Flutter client is now a **port of the canonical renderer**, not a re-implementation of the idea.
The reference is the web's renderer, which is pixel-identical to the archived original — so porting
*it* is porting the original.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `flutter_client/lib/features/intro/domain/intro_timeline.dart` | The cue constants, the easings, the source's `mulberry32` (with JavaScript's 32-bit semantics), the two seeded particle fields, and the deterministic camera shake |
| `flutter_client/lib/features/intro/domain/intro_frame.dart` | `computeIntroFrame(t)` — every animated value, rounded the way the canonical rounds it |
| `flutter_client/lib/features/intro/presentation/painters/intro_fx_painter.dart` | All seven FX and the sheen, with `BlendMode.plus` for the canvas's `lighter` |
| `flutter_client/lib/features/intro/presentation/painters/intro_veil_painter.dart` | The curtain: two halves of **one** continuous image |
| `flutter_client/lib/features/intro/presentation/widgets/brand_intro_overlay.dart` | The canonical layer stack, the responsive lockup, the handover, the removal at 3.00 s, and no Skip pill |
| `flutter_client/lib/features/intro/presentation/widgets/brand_mark.dart` | The canonical mark, path for path |
| `flutter_client/lib/features/intro/presentation/controllers/intro_controller.dart` | The deliberate gating |

**Every divergence the ledger named is closed:** L1 (the FX canvas is above the curtain), L2 (all
seven effects and the sheen), L3 (the grain), L4 (the responsive lockup), L5 (no Skip pill), L8 (the
curtain's geometry), L10 (the camera shake), L11 (reduced motion), L12 (the outro), L13 (the
gating), L16 (the mark) and L17 (the handover).

**And three things the ledger did not name, which the slice found:**

1. **The mark was wrong.** The specular highlight used `v 19.4 … L23.2 18.0` where the canonical's
   is `v8.5 l14.2-1.4 L12 9.2z` — roughly twice the area it should cover — and the shadow was a
   hard `drawShadow` at six units where the canonical has a soft `feDropShadow dy=2 stdDeviation=3`.
2. **The handover was missing entirely.** The overlay absorbed every touch for the whole three
   seconds, so the app underneath was not live until the sequence had gone.
3. **The curtain's gradient was wrong**, not only its geometry: the canonical is one stage-wide
   image carried by both halves, and the Flutter client had two independent radial blobs.

## 2. The decisions

| # | Decision | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| **D15** | The gating | **Keep "every launch", and say so.** The dead `hasSeenIntro` flag — written when the sequence finished, never persisted, never read — is gone, and the controller states the behaviour. The alternative was to persist it and play once per install; the specification does not ask for that. |
| **D16** | The handover | **Flutter releases touches at 2.57 s and keeps the keyboard for the whole three seconds**, so a D-pad press cannot navigate the app behind the sequence. The web releases both. |

## 3. The oracle

Two tiers, and the first is the load-bearing one.

**Tier 1 — the parameters, exact.** The canonical renderer is exported to
`docs/opening-sequence/frame-golden.json` (69 instants), recording the values it **rendered** —
parsed out of the very strings it wrote to the DOM, because the rounding is part of the rendering.
The Flutter client's frame is asserted against it, value for value, to 1 × 10⁻⁹.

**Tier 2 — the picture, perceptual.** The Flutter overlay is rendered at the archived frames' own
instants, at their own viewports and their own **pixel** sizes, and compared with the archived
original's frames over the closed-curtain window (58 of the 90 in each aspect). The thresholds were
**measured** over every frame and are recorded in the specification.

**The seeded fields are compared too.** Dart's 64-bit integers do not behave like JavaScript's
32-bit ones, so the port reimplements `Math.imul` and the `| 0` coercions, and the particles, the
embers and the shake are asserted against a captured reference — **bit for bit**.

### Three things the oracle caught that no listening test would have

- **The random draws were taken in the wrong order.** The first port of the particle fields read the
  PRNG inside a constructor's *named arguments* — and Dart does not promise that order — so every
  particle landed somewhere else.
- **The `toFixed` port rounded the wrong way.** Multiplying by the scale factor rounds, and a value
  just below a midpoint can land exactly on it: the curtain's feather at t = 2.57 came out one unit
  in the last place away from the canonical's. Dart's `toStringAsFixed` agrees with JavaScript's
  `toFixed` exactly — checked over **800,000** values.
- **The mark had no size.** A `CustomPaint` with no child and no size is zero-sized, so the
  canonical mark simply did not appear.

### And one that was in the harness, not in the port

The portrait's comparison first came out an order of magnitude worse than the landscape's, and the
cause was not the design: a widget test can only capture at the **logical** size, and the archived
portrait frames are 1080 × 1920 **pixels** (a 540 × 960 viewport at a device pixel ratio of 2) — so
the comparison was matching the top-left quarter of one frame with the whole of the other. The
frames are now compared at the logical scale, and the two aspects land in the same place.

## 4. The two recorded divergences

| # | What | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **L3** | The **grain** | The canonical's is an SVG `feTurbulence` filter, which Flutter cannot render — there is no SVG filter support. The Flutter client uses a **seeded white-noise** texture with the same tile size and the same colour matrix. The same *kind* of texture, not the same pixels. |
| **L17** | The **handover** | Flutter keeps the keyboard; the web releases it. D16. |

**One thing is excluded from the picture comparison, and it is a harness fact rather than a
divergence:** a Flutter widget test renders text with the **Ahem** test font, whose glyphs are solid
squares, so the wordmarks cannot be compared as pictures at all. The word column is excluded from
the comparison, and the text's *parameters* are pinned exactly by the frame fixture instead.

The three failures are the pre-existing `libmpv` ones (Issue 11) — `runtime_player_3a_test.dart`,
`runtime_player_3b_test.dart` and `app_shell_test.dart`, all failing in `setUpAll` — **unchanged and

**One thing is deliberately outstanding, and it is batched to the end of the phase: the
on-device check.** The exit criterion says "the phone and TV both show the canonical design", and
that is the one thing this slice cannot prove on the host — `adb devices` is empty, and
`app_shell_test.dart` (the file that would cover the AppShell-level integration) is one of the
three that cannot run here (Issue 11). So it is **one session at the end**, on the real hardware,
not a piecemeal attempt per slice. What it needs:

1. The APK built and installed on the **Fire TV Stick 4K** and the **phone**.
2. The intro triggered by a **cold launch** — it plays on *every* launch, by design (D15).
3. A screenshot of each, compared against the archived frames.
4. The owner's **remote-control protocol** observed: *"Please do not touch the remote..."* before
   any ADB key event, and *"The app is free from your control."* afterwards.
